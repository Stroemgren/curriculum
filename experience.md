# Experience

## Founder, Developer

**Demografix ApS** · January 2014 – Now

Since 2014 I’ve been running genderize.io, agify.io and nationalize.io. A niche service that provides statistics on names. It’s grown from a small project that was supposed to be just my own to a place where it delivers 200 requests per second on average. It’s used commercially for user analysis and in academia for research in gender equality. Customers have included Spotify, Uber, H&M and Podimo along with many universities, and it’s been mentioned in The Guardian and The Washington Post.

At the same time, it’s always been my personal learning space and it’s been through multiple rewrites. Today it’s running Elixir, backed by a Postgres database.

Aside from a bit of bookkeeping I can run it passively while working and then I’ll usually take time between jobs for some TLC.

## Consultant, AI R&D

**Sea by Maritech, Clarksons** · October 2024 – March 2026

In 2024, I brought a prototype to my former employer, Sea by Maritech. Microsoft had just released an open source project that attempted to force LLMs to deliver structured output based on TypeScript types (time moves fast :D). Sea by Maritech is a platform for the shipping industry and holds a quarter of a million charter parties (shipping contracts). I saw the value in being able to read the contracts as structured data.

Over a year and a half I developed the project on my own while learning enough about charter parties to actually interpret them. Towards the end we built an internal team that took over and put it into production. The structured contract data is now sold back to the customers who hold their charter parties on the platform, and internal work has begun on building freight rate indices and other downstream analytics on top of it.

## Senior Technical Architect

**Sea by Maritech, Clarksons** · November 2022 – October 2023

Sea by Maritech is based in London, but shortly before I arrived the entire management team had been replaced and the new leadership sat in Denmark. I knew the incoming CTO well, and he brought me in because he needed someone on the ground who could work out what was actually going on. The organisation was remote, newly reorganised, and not many people spoke up.

I was embedded as an architect across most of the engineering teams, which gave me a view of what was working and what wasn’t. Part of my job was reporting that back to the CTO. The other part was helping the organisation run smoother. Some of the biggest blockers for engineering turned out to be legal, infosec and devops, so I ended up pushing on those. More directly, I ran an API working group that set company-wide standards and I was part of the decision to sunset an existing application, Trade, and build its successor. We formed a new team for that, which I ran until I left.

## Tech Lead

**Good Monday** · January 2020 – June 2022

Good Monday was a startup of around 25 people that took care of offices. Cleaning, catering, employee gifts, keeping the fridge and the paper stocked, parties, whatever an office needed. We didn’t do any of the work ourselves but worked with suppliers, and the value for the customer was having one supplier to talk to instead of twenty, plus a customer care team that knew them.

We built everything in house. The customer facing platform, the internal customer care platform and the billing system. I started as an engineer and fairly early took over responsibility for the engineering team, around 8 engineers, when the CTO (the same one who later brought me to Sea by Maritech) wanted to focus on product and design.

Good Monday didn’t survive Covid and closed in 2022.
